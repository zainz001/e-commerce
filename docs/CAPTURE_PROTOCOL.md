# Capture protocol

Status: proposed pilot protocol. Review this protocol before collecting real data.

## Pilot target

Capture adult consenting participants wearing exact stitched Pakistani straight-cut suits/kameez with trousers. Do not record minors. Do not make claims about size or physical fit. The initial pilot should avoid a moving dupatta; record its presence if it appears and flag the take for scope review.

Capture the garment as it is naturally worn for the participant. The target is a truthful visual reference for how the selected design appears on that person, including its silhouette, drape, details, and continuity during movement. Do not tighten, loosen, pin, retouch, or otherwise alter footage to create an artificial fit claim.

## Before the session

1. Assign opaque `person_id`, `garment_id`, `garment_family_id`, and `session_id` values. Keep identity-to-contact information in restricted storage only.
2. Verify the garment owner or supplier has permission for the product images and commercial model-training use.
3. Obtain participant permission covering capture, storage, quality review, model-training research/development, commercial training if intended, sharing limits, retention, and a clear withdrawal contact/process.
4. Remove bystanders and unnecessary personal information from the scene. Do not capture exact addresses or location metadata in derived material.
5. Prepare a static camera, uncluttered background, even lighting, and enough space for the whole intended garment to remain visible.

## Product media

For each exact garment, capture or obtain clearly linked product images: front, back, and close-ups of print/embroidery, neckline, sleeves, and other important visible details. A flat-lay or mannequin image is useful when feasible. Preserve true colors and retain the product/SKU linkage. Missing views remain missing; they must not be generated and labeled as observations.

## Participant reference

Capture a front reference image or optional short clip of the same consenting adult in comfortable ordinary neutral clothing. No revealing baseline clothing is required. Keep face, hair, skin tone, and general visible appearance clear only with the participant's permission.

## Wearing takes

Record the same participant actually wearing the exact photographed garment. A practical initial target is 1080p at approximately 25–30 fps and 10–20 seconds per take when supported. Preserve the device's original resolution and frame rate; never silently resample or fabricate source frames.

Use separate, labeled takes for:

- front neutral pose;
- gentle left turn and right turn;
- optional arms lifting or crossing;
- approach/step back;
- optional short continuous turn when safe.

Include stationary intervals and natural-speed movement. Record camera orientation, lighting/background group, visible occlusion, whether the full intended garment stays in frame, and any interruptions or retakes.

## After capture

- Copy originals without editing, transcoding, or overwriting them.
- Record source filename, capture date/time where appropriate, device/orientation notes, and checksum after copying.
- Keep consent/rights records separate from public manifests.
- Inspect readability and coverage before accepting a take as a candidate.
- Strip unnecessary geotags and EXIF from derived material only; retain technical metadata needed for QA.
- Quarantine a withdrawal or rights issue and its derivatives; do not silently delete evidence or claim that trained models automatically forget a withdrawn source.
